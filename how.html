Behind the scenes

How ORBIT works

Every chart on ORBIT is calculated from real astronomy, live, the moment you ask for it. Nothing is made up or copied from a list. Here’s where each part comes from.

From your birth details to your chart

  1. 01

    You

    type your date, time and place of birth.

  2. 02

    Open-Meteo

    finds your birthplace: its exact location and time zone.

  3. 03

    Your browser

    works out the exact moment you were born, worldwide.

  4. 04

    Astronomy Engine

    calculates where the Sun, Moon and planets were.

  5. 05

    ORBIT

    adds the signs, houses and angles, and explains them in plain words.

01 · Where you were born

The place search

When you type your city, ORBIT asks Open-Meteo, a free and open place-search service, for places with that name. Each result comes with the place’s exact position on Earth (latitude and longitude) and its time zone.

You have to pick your city from the list, so the place is always a real one. The city name you type is the only thing sent: never your date or time of birth.

In the code: js/astro/places.js

02 · The exact moment

Time zones

“14:30 in Madrid” isn’t the same moment as “14:30 in Lima”, and clocks change with summer time, and countries have changed their rules over the years.

Your browser already contains the world’s time-zone history (the IANA time zone database). ORBIT uses it to find what the clocks read in your birthplace on your birth date, and turns your birth time into one exact moment that’s the same everywhere (UTC).

In the code: js/astro/time.js

03 · Where the planets were

The astronomy

Astronomy Engine is a free, open-source astronomy library by Don Cross. For that exact moment, it calculates where the Sun, Moon and each planet appeared in the sky, as seen from Earth. It’s accurate to about 1/60 of a degree, far finer than you could ever see on the wheel.

It runs inside the website itself, so your birth details are never sent anywhere to be calculated.

In the code: js/vendor/astronomy.js (the library) and js/astro/chart.js

04 · ORBIT’s own calculations

Signs, houses and angles

  • Signs: each planet’s position is placed in one of the 12 signs, using the .
  • Rising sign and houses: worked out from your birth time and place with the method. This part was written by hand for ORBIT.
  • Angles between planets (): ORBIT measures the distance between every pair of planets and looks for the five special angles.

No birth time? Then there’s no Rising sign or houses, because they change every few hours. ORBIT leaves them out rather than guess. An approximate time? ORBIT shows every option that time could give.

In the code: js/astro/zodiac.js, js/astro/houses.js and js/astro/aspects.js

05 · Checked against real charts

Accuracy tests

ORBIT is tested against charts published by astrology websites (Astrotheme and Astro-Charts) for well-known people, including Barack Obama and Rafael Nadal. 83 automatic checks compare every planet, the Rising sign and the houses, plus tricky time-zone cases.

Each planet has to match within 2/60 of a degree (the fast-moving Moon within 3/60). The tests run before any change to the calculations is saved.

In the code: development/tests/

06 · The words

The explanations

The meanings come from traditional astrology, written in plain words for people who are just starting out. They were drafted with the help of AI and edited by Salma, ORBIT’s creator.

Every reading is built from your own placements, using astrology’s simple formula: the planet is what part of you, the sign is how it shows up, and the house is where in your life. So two different charts always get different readings.

The positions are real astronomy. Their meanings are astrology: a tradition of interpretation, not science or prediction.

In the code: js/readings.js and the files in js/content/

07 · The sky now

This week and time travel

“Your sky this week” and “Travel through time” use the same astronomy, run for today or any day you choose. ORBIT then compares that sky with your birth chart and explains the closest angles.

In the code: js/astro/sky.js and js/time-travel.js

08 · Your data

Where it’s kept

Your details are saved in your own browser. If you create an account, they’re also kept in Supabase, a secure online database, so they follow you to any device. Its rules let each person read and change only their own data.

You can download everything or delete it at any time on your Profile.

In the code: js/store.js and js/cloud.js
