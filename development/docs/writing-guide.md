# ORBIT writing guide

**The rule for the whole website:** write for someone who is learning about astrology for the first time. Technical words are allowed, but every one of them is explained in plain words, right where it appears. Nobody should ever need to look something up somewhere else, or ask another AI what a sentence means.

---

## 1. Meaning first, term second

Say what something *is* before (or alongside) its name.

- ✗ "Your Ascendant is in Scorpio."
- ✓ "How you come across to others — your **Rising sign** — is Scorpio."

## 2. Explain every astrology word the first time it appears on a page

Every technical word gets a short, plain explanation the first time it shows up on a page: in brackets, in the same sentence, or as a hover/tap definition (dotted underline). The same word always gets the same explanation everywhere, so definitions live in one shared list (the glossary) that the whole site reads from.

Words that must **always** be explained:

| Word | Plain explanation |
| --- | --- |
| Birth chart (carta astral) | A map of where the planets were in the sky at the moment and place you were born |
| Sign | One of the 12 zodiac signs. In a chart, it describes *how* something shows up |
| Planet | In astrology, a part of your personality (the Sun, the Moon and the planets). It describes *what* |
| House | One of 12 areas of life, like home, love or work. It describes *where* |
| Rising sign / Ascendant | The sign that was rising on the horizon when you were born. It's about how you come across |
| Midheaven (MC) | The highest point of the sky when you were born. It's about your direction and public life |
| Aspect | The angle between two planets, which shows how they "talk" to each other |
| Conjunction, sextile, square, trine, opposition | The five main angles (0°, 60°, 90°, 120°, 180°): blended, friendly, tense, easy, pulled in two directions |
| Orb | How close an angle is to exact. The smaller, the stronger |
| Degree (14°) | How far a planet is through its sign, out of 30 |
| Retrograde | When a planet *looks* like it's moving backwards from Earth. Traditionally a time to slow down and look inward |
| Transit | Where a planet is in the sky *now*, compared with your birth chart |
| Cusp | Where a house (or sign) begins |
| Natal | Another word for "birth", as in natal chart |
| Placidus | The method ORBIT uses to divide the chart into houses (there are several; this is the most common) |
| Tropical zodiac | The way ORBIT measures signs, starting from the first day of spring |

Prefer the everyday word when there is one: "comparing charts", not "synastry"; "birth chart", not "natal chart"; "the planets this week", not "current transits". Use the technical word *as well* (explained), so users learn it, but never *instead*.

## 3. Short and plain

- Short sentences. One idea per sentence.
- Everyday words. If a 14-year-old wouldn't get it, rewrite it.
- No walls of text. A short version always comes first; the long reading is optional.
- Numbers always come with what they mean: "14° Scorpio (about halfway through the sign)".

## 4. Personal, never generic

Every reading uses the person's actual placements, and how they combine: planet + sign + house + the planets it connects to. "Scorpios are intense" is generic. "Your Venus in Scorpio sits in your 8th house and clashes with your Mars" is about *you*.

## 5. Honest tone

- Astrology is presented as a tradition of interpretation, not as facts or predictions: "In astrology, the Moon represents…", "This placement is traditionally linked with…".
- No fortune-telling ("you will…"), no scores, no scare words.
- If something can't be known (no birth time), say so plainly and say why.

- [ ] Does the short version come first?
- [ ] Is it about this person's chart, not their sign in general?
